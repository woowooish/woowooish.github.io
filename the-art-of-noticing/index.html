01 / THE IDEA

No special setting required.
A kitchen counts.

✺

Maybe the extraordinary has a very ordinary address.

The light on a kitchen wall. A familiar song heard with new ears. The small relief of someone making room for you.

There is a kind of wonder that asks us to go somewhere impressive. Book the retreat. Find the perfect beach. Become the sort of person who remembers to journal before checking their phone.

This is an invitation to try something smaller. Not because big experiences are wrong, but because an ordinary Tuesday deserves a little attention, too.

You can be curious about consciousness and still wonder what to make for dinner. You can love the idea of magic and ask sensible questions. You can notice something beautiful while having a thoroughly un-beautiful day. None of those things cancels the others out.

Here, noticing means pausing long enough to meet one thing before turning it into a task, an explanation or a photograph. What is actually here? What does it bring up? What can remain unfinished?

Not every moment needs a lesson. The leaf can just be a leaf. The coincidence can be interesting without becoming an instruction. A warm cup can be a warm cup, which is already quite a nice thing.

Call it presence, ordinary attention, or a little of the woo-wooness of the world. The name can stay loose. Start with what is in front of you.

02 / YOUR WAY IN

One small invitation.
Right where you are.

Choose a setting, not a better version of yourself. A minute is enough. So is stopping when you have had enough.

Not a reading. Not a test.
Just somewhere to rest your attention.

AT HOME · INVITATION 1 OF 3

Meet an ordinary object.

Choose a cup, a key, a folded shirt or something else nearby. Notice one detail you usually skip: its weight, a worn edge, the way the light meets it. You do not have to find a deeper meaning. Let the detail be enough.

What was here before you thought to look?

Browse all nine invitations, at your own pace

At home

  1. Meet an ordinary object. Notice a familiar thing’s weight, texture or worn edge. What have you been passing by?
  2. Find a patch of light. Look at light on a wall or an object, not directly at the sun. Notice its shape and edges. What changes while you are here?
  3. Let one sound arrive. Notice a nearby sound without needing to identify every sound around you. What happens when you do not have to search for something special?

Outside

  1. Look at the in-between. From a safe place, notice the space between branches, buildings or clouds. What comes into view when you look at the gaps?
  2. Notice a small change. Stay still somewhere comfortable. Watch a shadow, a leaf or the surface of water. What is moving without needing you to move it?
  3. Leave the photograph untaken. Give one small scene a moment before reaching for your phone. You can still take a photograph afterward. What would you remember without one?

With someone

  1. Ask a smaller question. Try: “What is one small thing you noticed today?” Leave room for an ordinary answer. What would you have missed without asking?
  2. Notice an act of care. Look for something concrete, not a hidden message: a held door, a checked-in text, a cup made for someone. What did that action make room for?
  3. Let a moment be unfilled. With someone comfortable with a little quiet, let a pause stay a pause. No need to manufacture intimacy. What does company look like without performing?

Use whichever senses are available and comfortable. Looking, listening, touch and imagination are options, not a checklist. Stay somewhere safe; there is no need to close your eyes or change your breathing.

03 / FIVE FIELD NOTES

Keep the wonder.
Lose the homework.

Open whichever note meets you today. There is no correct order and nothing to complete.

01Borrow a beginner’s eyes.+

Familiarity can become a label we stop looking beyond. “My street.” “That tree.” “The same old view.” Try setting the label down for a moment. There may be a crooked branch, a new color or an unexpectedly generous patch of sky underneath it.

You are not required to make the familiar exciting. This is simply an invitation to let it be specific again. Even “I had never noticed that scratch” counts.

A question to carry: What would I notice here if I had not already decided what this place was?

02Let two feelings belong.+

A lovely view does not have to fix a hard day. Gratitude does not need to escort disappointment out of the room. You can be glad about one thing and unsettled about another.

Try a sentence with “and” in the middle: “I am tired, and this light is beautiful.” Or “I do not know what comes next, and this cup is warm.” Keep the two halves small and true. No silver lining is required.

A question to carry: What else can be true without making this feeling wrong?

03Be interested before being certain.+

Something catches your attention: a repeated word, a strangely well-timed song, a coincidence you want to tell someone about. You can enjoy the feeling of “huh, interesting” without immediately deciding what caused it.

One useful distinction is between a moment being meaningful to you and that moment proving something about the world. The first does not automatically establish the second. You can hold a possible interpretation gently, alongside ordinary explanations and the possibility that you do not know.

A question to carry: What did I actually observe, and what am I adding to it?

Link to this note ↗
04Notice care, then make a little.+

Wonder does not have to arrive as a spectacular sky. It might look like someone remembering the small thing you said, or leaving the last clean mug for another person.

Rather than looking for a grand sign that love exists, try noticing a specific action. Then choose one small, welcome action of your own: a thank-you that says what mattered, an offer with no pressure attached, or giving someone room to say no. Care does not need to become a performance.

A question to carry: What would kindness look like here if it did not need to look impressive?

Link to this note ↗
05Leave one question open.+

What does it mean to feel connected? Why can an ordinary moment feel suddenly vivid? How much of what we call magic is in the world, and how much is in the way we meet it?

You do not have to settle those questions before going for a walk, making lunch or being kind to someone. Write down the question that interests you. Let “I am still wondering” be a full sentence, rather than a failure to reach a conclusion.

A question to carry: Which question would I like to keep company with, rather than solve today?

04 / THE “ISH” IS IMPORTANT

Room for mystery.
Feet on the ground.

A small moment can feel meaningful without becoming a message you must obey.

The “ish” leaves room to be moved without pretending to know more than you do. It leaves room for spiritual curiosity and ordinary explanations to sit at the same table.

Try keeping three things separate: what happened, what it brought up in you, and the meaning you are considering. None needs to impersonate the others.

Especially when a decision has real consequences, give it practical attention, relevant evidence and advice you can trust. A coincidence does not have to make the decision for you.

AN IMAGINED EXAMPLE, NOT ANNIE’S PERSONAL STORY
01 / OBSERVATION

What happened?

“A song came on just as I was thinking about an old friend.”

02 / EXPERIENCE

What did it bring up?

“I felt surprised, a little nostalgic, and glad to remember them.”

03 / INTERPRETATION

What am I wondering?

“It felt like a nudge to say hello. I do not know why the timing lined up.”

You can choose to send a kind hello because you want to. You do not need certainty about the universe to make a human gesture.

06 / TAKE IT INTO YOUR WEEK

Seven possibilities.
Not a seven-day challenge.

Start anywhere. Skip, repeat, or stop. There is no streak to protect.

  1. 01

    A color

    Notice one shade you would not have thought to name.

  2. 02

    A sound

    Let a familiar sound be more than background for a moment.

  3. 03

    A kindness

    Name one specific thing someone did that mattered.

  4. 04

    A texture

    Notice a comfortable, familiar surface with a little more attention.

  5. 05

    A question

    Write down something you are curious about, without answering it.

  6. 06

    A both-and

    Let two true things share a sentence.

  7. 07

    A return

    Look at something from earlier in the week. What do you notice now?
